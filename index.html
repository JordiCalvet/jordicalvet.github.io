Jordi Calvet-Bademunt

Senior Research Fellow, The Future of Free Speech, Vanderbilt University
Visiting Legal Researcher, Barcelona Supercomputing Center
Washington, D.C.

I am a lawyer and policy researcher working on AI governance, risk management, and fundamental rights. My work asks two connected questions: how frontier AI systems actually behave when people use them, and whether the rules being written for those systems are the right ones.

At The Future of Free Speech I designed and lead the organization's flagship research program on AI and freedom of expression, with a budget over $1 million and a team of ten legal experts. We evaluate ten major AI models, including those built by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta, and assess AI governance across twelve jurisdictions — among them the EU, the United States, China, Brazil, India, and South Korea. I take the findings to the people writing the rules, through bilateral meetings with senior officials at the European Commission and the European Parliament and regular consultations with industry.

I serve on the European Commission's working group drafting the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice under the EU AI Act, and I am a member of the Freedom Online Coalition's Advisory Network, one of roughly 30 experts selected worldwide to advise an intergovernmental coalition of more than 40 democratic governments. Anthropic invited me as one of three external organizations to review one of its models. I am completing a part-time Ph.D. at ESADE Law School on systemic-risk obligations under the EU AI Act and the Digital Services Act.

Before AI policy, I spent four years as a policy analyst in the OECD's Competition Division in Paris, leading reform projects with the governments of Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Tunisia, and before that practiced antitrust and EU law at Uría Menéndez. I hold an M.P.A. from the Harvard Kennedy School and an LL.M. from the College of Europe.
