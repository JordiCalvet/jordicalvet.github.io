Jordi Calvet-Bademunt

Senior Research Fellow, The Future of Free Speech, Vanderbilt University

I am a lawyer and policy researcher at The Future of Free Speech at Vanderbilt University, working on AI governance, risk management, and fundamental rights. My work considers how AI governance affects fundamental rights, particularly through risk-based regulation, and how AI models behave when people use them.

At Vanderbilt, I designed and lead a research program on AI and freedom of expression, with a budget over $1 million and a team of more than ten legal and policy experts. We evaluate ten major AI models, including those built by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta, and assess AI governance across twelve jurisdictions — among them the EU, the United States, China, Brazil, India, and South Korea. I bring the findings to policymakers, industry, and the public: through commentary in outlets from MSNBC to Lawfare, through conferences I speak at, and through direct advice to regulators and developers.

I am a member of the Freedom Online Coalition's Advisory Network, one of 30 experts selected worldwide to advise an intergovernmental coalition of more than 40 governments. Anthropic invited my team at Vanderbilt as one of three external organizations to review one of its models. I am completing a part-time Ph.D. at ESADE Law School on systemic-risk obligations under the EU AI Act and the Digital Services Act.

Before joining Vanderbilt, I spent four years as a policy analyst at the OECD in Paris, leading reform projects with the governments of Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Tunisia, and before that practiced as an attorney in EU law for five years in leading European law firms. I hold an M.P.A. from the Harvard Kennedy School and an LL.M. from the College of Europe.
